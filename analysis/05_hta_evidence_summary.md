## Research Question 1 — MCI Due to Alzheimer's Disease

### PICO

**Population**

Patients with mild cognitive impairment due to Alzheimer's disease meeting the relevant indication criteria.

**Intervention**

Lecanemab.

**Comparator**

Watchful waiting.

**Key outcomes**

- Mortality
- Disease progression
- Cognitive outcomes
- Health-related quality of life
- Adverse events

### Interpretation

The relevant analyses did not demonstrate a statistically significant advantage for lecanemab on the evaluated efficacy and quality-of-life outcomes.

At the same time, infusion-related reactions occurred significantly more frequently with lecanemab.

Therefore, the evidence did not establish an added benefit.

---

## Research Question 2 — Mild Alzheimer's Dementia With AChEI Therapy

### PICO

**Population**

Patients with mild Alzheimer's dementia receiving acetylcholinesterase inhibitor therapy.

**Intervention**

Lecanemab in addition to AChEI therapy.

**Comparator**

Placebo in addition to AChEI therapy.

### Relevant population

The relevant subgroup included:

- Lecanemab + AChEI: n = 139
- Placebo + AChEI: n = 138

### Key efficacy findings

| Outcome | Relative effect | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| CDR-SB deterioration | RR 0.84 [0.56–1.27] | No statistically significant difference |
| ADAS-Cog14 deterioration | RR 0.63 [0.35–1.14] | No statistically significant difference |
| EQ-5D VAS deterioration | RR 0.66 [0.38–1.17] | No statistically significant difference |
| QOL-AD deterioration | RR 0.56 [0.28–1.15] | No statistically significant difference |

### Safety

Infusion-related reactions:

**RR 3.18 [1.63–6.21], p<0.001**

This represents a statistically significant increase in infusion-related reactions with lecanemab.

### Interpretation

The relevant evidence did not establish a statistically significant efficacy or quality-of-life advantage over the appropriate comparator.

The safety analysis identified increased infusion-related reactions.

Therefore, an added benefit was not proven.

---

## Research Question 3 — Mild Alzheimer's Dementia Without AChEI Therapy

### PICO

**Population**

Patients with mild Alzheimer's dementia.

**Intervention**

Lecanemab monotherapy.

**Comparator**

Acetylcholinesterase inhibitor therapy.

### Evidence

No relevant comparative study was identified that directly addressed this treatment situation.

### Interpretation

This is an evidence gap.

The appropriate conclusion is not:

> Lecanemab does not work as monotherapy.

Instead:

> No relevant comparative evidence was available to establish an added-benefit assessment for this treatment situation.

This distinction is important when interpreting evidence gaps in HTA.
