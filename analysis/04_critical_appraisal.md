# 04 — Critical Appraisal

## Purpose

The purpose of this section is to critically evaluate the evidence used in the IQWiG assessment rather than simply reproduce the published conclusions.

The appraisal focuses on:

1. Comparator alignment
2. Population relevance
3. Statistical evidence
4. Precision and uncertainty
5. Risk of bias
6. Benefit-harm balance
7. Evidence gaps

---

## 1. Comparator Alignment

A central issue in German HTA is whether the comparator used in the clinical evidence corresponds to the defined Zweckmäßige Vergleichstherapie (ZVT).

For the IQWiG A25-111 assessment, the relevant comparators differed by research question.

### MCI due to Alzheimer's disease

The defined ZVT was:

**Watchful waiting (beobachtendes Abwarten).**

The relevant analysis therefore needed to reflect this treatment situation.

### Mild Alzheimer's dementia

The defined ZVT was:

**Acetylcholinesterase inhibitor therapy.**

The relevant comparison was therefore:

**Lecanemab + AChEI vs placebo + AChEI.**

This distinction is important because results from the overall CLARITY AD population cannot automatically be interpreted as evidence of added benefit against the German ZVT.

---

## 2. Population Relevance

The CLARITY AD trial included a broader population than the specific German HTA research questions.

Therefore, the assessment required identification of relevant subpopulations.

For mild Alzheimer's dementia, the relevant population consisted of patients receiving acetylcholinesterase inhibitor therapy at baseline.

The relevant comparison included:

- Lecanemab + AChEI: n = 139
- Placebo + AChEI: n = 138

This illustrates an important HTA principle:

> Evidence must be relevant not only to the disease indication but also to the specific population and treatment setting defined by the assessment.

---

## 3. Statistical Evidence

The relevant efficacy and health-related quality-of-life outcomes did not demonstrate a statistically significant advantage for lecanemab over the appropriate comparator.

Examples from the mild Alzheimer's dementia population receiving AChEI therapy include:

| Outcome | Effect estimate | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| CDR-SB deterioration | RR 0.84 [0.56–1.27], p=0.410 | No statistically significant difference |
| ADAS-Cog14 deterioration | RR 0.63 [0.35–1.14], p=0.130 | No statistically significant difference |
| EQ-5D VAS deterioration | RR 0.66 [0.38–1.17], p=0.155 | No statistically significant difference |
| QOL-AD deterioration | RR 0.56 [0.28–1.15], p=0.115 | No statistically significant difference |

The results therefore do not provide statistical evidence of an efficacy or quality-of-life advantage in the relevant ZVT-conforming population.

---

## 4. Precision and Uncertainty

Statistical significance alone is not sufficient for evidence appraisal.

The confidence interval also provides information about the precision of the estimate.

For example:

**CDR-SB deterioration**

RR = 0.84  
95% CI = 0.56–1.27

The confidence interval crosses the null value of 1.

Therefore, the result does not demonstrate a statistically significant difference.

More importantly, the interval remains sufficiently broad that the underlying effect is uncertain.

This illustrates why effect estimates should be interpreted together with their confidence intervals rather than using p-values alone.

---

## 5. Safety Evidence

The assessment must consider both potential benefits and potential harms.

In the mild Alzheimer's dementia population receiving AChEI therapy, infusion-related reactions occurred more frequently with lecanemab.

The reported estimate was:

**RR 3.18 [1.63–6.21], p<0.001**

This represents a statistically significant increase in infusion-related reactions.

The finding is important because an HTA assessment does not ask only whether a medicine improves disease-related outcomes.

It also evaluates whether treatment introduces clinically relevant harms.

---

## 6. Risk of Bias

IQWiG assessed the relevant analyses as having a high risk of bias.

The assessment identified methodological limitations affecting the interpretation of the evidence, including issues relating to:

- treatment modifications
- disease progression
- treatment discontinuation
- loss of relevant treatment exposure

A high risk of bias increases uncertainty regarding the estimated treatment effects.

Therefore, even where numerical differences are observed, the methodological quality of the underlying evidence must be considered before drawing conclusions about added benefit.

---

## 7. Absence of Evidence vs Evidence of No Effect

An important distinction in this assessment concerns the difference between:

**No demonstrated effect**

and

**No evidence available.**

For several outcomes, comparative evidence existed but did not demonstrate a statistically significant advantage for lecanemab.

This should not automatically be interpreted as proof that lecanemab has no effect.

In contrast, for mild Alzheimer's dementia treated with lecanemab monotherapy, IQWiG identified no relevant comparative study against the appropriate ZVT.

This represents an evidence gap.

Therefore:

> Lack of relevant evidence should not be described as evidence that the treatment is ineffective.

---

## 8. Benefit-Harm Assessment

The assessment illustrates why an HTA conclusion cannot be based on a single efficacy endpoint.

The relevant evidence contains:

### Potential benefits

No statistically significant advantage was demonstrated for the relevant efficacy and quality-of-life outcomes in the ZVT-conforming analyses.

### Potential harms

Infusion-related reactions occurred significantly more frequently with lecanemab.

### Uncertainty

The relevant analyses were assessed as having a high risk of bias, while some estimates had wide confidence intervals.

The overall evidence therefore requires simultaneous consideration of:

- efficacy
- safety
- methodological quality
- precision
- comparator relevance
- evidence gaps

---

## 9. Overall Interpretation

The relevant evidence did not establish an added benefit for lecanemab in the assessed ZVT-conforming treatment situations.

The analysis demonstrates that the conclusion cannot be reached simply by asking whether the overall CLARITY AD trial produced a positive result.

Instead, the evidence must be filtered through the specific German HTA question.

The analytical sequence is:

**Clinical trial**

↓

**Relevant population**

↓

**German ZVT**

↓

**Relevant comparison**

↓

**Patient-relevant outcomes**

↓

**Statistical analysis**

↓

**Risk of bias and uncertainty**

↓

**Benefit-harm assessment**

↓

**Added-benefit conclusion**

---

## Key Takeaway

The most important lesson from this case is:

> A clinical trial result is not automatically equivalent to a German HTA added-benefit result.

The relevance of the evidence depends on population, comparator, outcomes, methodological quality and uncertainty.

This is particularly important in Market Access because reimbursement decisions are based on the evidence relevant to the specific healthcare system and comparator framework.
