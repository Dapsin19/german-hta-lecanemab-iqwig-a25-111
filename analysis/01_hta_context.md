# 01 — German HTA Context

## §35a SGB V

Section 35a of the German Social Code Book V (SGB V) establishes the framework for the early benefit assessment of medicinal products with new active ingredients.

The assessment determines whether a new medicine provides an additional benefit compared with the appropriate comparator therapy.

---

## AMNOG

The early benefit assessment forms part of the German pharmaceutical reimbursement framework introduced through the Arzneimittelmarktneuordnungsgesetz (AMNOG).

The general process involves:

1. Marketing authorisation
2. Submission of a manufacturer dossier
3. Early benefit assessment
4. Scientific assessment
5. Commenting procedure
6. G-BA decision
7. Reimbursement/pricing consequences

---

## IQWiG

The Institut für Qualität und Wirtschaftlichkeit im Gesundheitswesen (IQWiG) is responsible for scientific assessments in many German HTA procedures.

IQWiG evaluates the available evidence according to its methodological framework.

Its assessment should be distinguished from the formal decision made by the Gemeinsamer Bundesausschuss (G-BA).

---

## G-BA

The Gemeinsame Bundesausschuss (G-BA) is the highest decision-making body of the joint self-government of physicians, dentists, hospitals and health insurance funds in Germany.

In the §35a process, the G-BA makes the formal decision concerning the extent of added benefit.

---

## Why This Matters for Market Access

The German HTA process requires more than demonstrating that a medicine works in a clinical trial.

The evidence must answer a specific reimbursement question.

That requires alignment between:

- the patient population
- the intervention
- the comparator
- relevant outcomes
- evidence quality
- uncertainty
- benefit and harm

This makes evidence interpretation and critical appraisal central skills in Market Access.
